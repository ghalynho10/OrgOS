# Q20 in SQL, clause by clause

Question: who are the top 5 people by total logged hours across multiple projects
between July 1 and September 30, 2009 (UTC), ranked from most to least?

Both files read `model.logged_time` (one row per time entry: `history_id`, `person_key`,
`issue_key`, signed `seconds`, and `logged_at`, the history's `created` converted to UTC
from its own offset, spec 0006 AC-18) and `model.issue` (`issue_key`, `project_key`).

## The rulings this pair follows

Your rulings of 2026-11-02, recorded in the body of main repo commit `25e339a`: ruling 2,
`project` is listed and holds the projects of the listed issues and entries; ruling 3,
`person` is listed, returned people only, and Q20's one step lists `issue`, `person`,
`project` and `time_entry`. The accepted plan: the scope is the seven projects, with the
other conditions inside it. The standing sources rule is in commits `2ecf29c` and
`b13f405`.

## `Q20.answer.sql`

- `entry`: **step 1** and **step 2**. `INNER JOIN model.issue` gives each entry its one
  issue's current project, so no entry is repeated (checklist item 5). `project_key IN
  (...)` is the seven project population. `logged_at >= TIMESTAMPTZ '2009-07-01
  00:00:00+00' AND logged_at < TIMESTAMPTZ '2009-10-01 00:00:00+00'` is the half open
  interval `[start, end)` with an explicit `+00` offset, so an entry at midnight on
  October 1 is out (checklist item 2). `seconds > 0` keeps positive entries (checklist
  item 7). The flagged bot set is empty (spec 0006 AC-17) and every entry has an author
  (spec 0006 AC-16), so no bot or author clause is written.
- `totals`: `HAVING count(DISTINCT e.project_key) >= 2` is **step 3**, at least two
  distinct projects among the author's qualifying entries in the same interval; `sum(
  e.seconds)` is **step 4**, the author's qualifying amounts across the seven projects,
  each entry once.
- `ranked` and `WHERE r.place <= 5`: **step 5**, rank highest first with `rank()`, under
  which tied totals share a place, so `place <= 5` keeps the top five and every person
  tied with the fifth, or all of them when fewer than five qualify. The `5` is the
  question's own number (checklist item 10).
- `ORDER BY r.seconds DESC, r.person_key`: rank order, with ties in key order.

The Q20 adapter turns each row, in the query's order, into `(person:<person_key>,
seconds)`. It adds and drops nothing.

## `Q20.sources.1.sql`

The known answer has one source step, with kinds `issue`, `person`, `project` and
`time_entry` (ruling 3).

- `entry` and `ranked`: the same conditions and cut as the answer (**step 1** to
  **step 5**).
- The final `SELECT DISTINCT ... WHERE r.place <= 5`: every qualifying entry of each
  returned person, the issue it is on, that issue's project and the person. Only the
  returned people's entries are listed, so no other author appears as a person
  (ruling 3), and the projects are those of the listed issues and entries (ruling 2),
  which are each returned person's distinct qualifying projects. The columns repeat per
  row and are read as sets. No cell is NULL.

## Checklist notes

Item 3: the answer sums seconds, which the question asks for, and no group is empty.
Item 4: a person with fewer than two projects is outside the ranking by the note's own
rule, so no zero row is owed. Item 6 does not arise: no key used here can be missing or
empty. No path is built (item 9). Numbers written: the dates, the `2` of "multiple
projects" and the `5`, all from the question or reading note (item 10).

## The difference from the Cypher pair

None in meaning. The graph stores `loggedAt` in the `Z` form and bounds it with
`datetime('...Z')`, where SQL uses a `+00` offset. SQL cuts with `rank() <= 5`; the graph
sorts, takes the fifth total (or the last when fewer than five qualify) and keeps every
total at or above it, the same set.
